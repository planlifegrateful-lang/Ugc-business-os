# 7-Day Discipline Reset
## A Practical Muslim Guide to Rebuilding Your Daily Routine
**Plan Life Grateful**

---

## Before you begin

This guide is a practical planning aid. It is not a religious ruling, a substitute for qualified Islamic guidance, or a promise that every day will go perfectly. Adapt the exercises to your circumstances and responsibilities.

The aim is not to become perfect in seven days. The aim is to make one sincere, manageable improvement and learn how to repeat it.

### Your reset rules
1. Keep the plan small enough to do on a difficult day.
2. Choose actions you control; do not measure yourself by outcomes you cannot control.
3. When you miss a step, restart at the next opportunity instead of abandoning the whole plan.
4. Make room for salah, family and community responsibilities, rest, and lawful work.
5. End each day with an honest review, not self-insults.

## Day 1 — Set your intention

**Today's focus:** Decide what you want this reset to serve.

Write a short answer:
- What matters most to me right now?
- Which part of my daily routine needs attention first?
- Why is improving this important to me and the people I serve?

**Choose one priority:** __________________________________

**The smallest useful step I can take today:** ______________________________

**Gratitude note:** Name one blessing you do not want to take for granted.

**Evening review:** Did I take the small step? If not, what made it difficult, and how can I make it smaller tomorrow?

---

## Day 2 — Choose daily anchors

**Today's focus:** Give the day a few reliable points of structure.

Choose up to three anchors that fit your life. Examples: prepare for the next salah, review the day's responsibilities, take care of one essential task, or prepare tomorrow's first step.

1. Anchor: __________________________ When: __________________
2. Anchor: __________________________ When: __________________
3. Anchor: __________________________ When: __________________

Do not fill every hour. A useful plan leaves room for unexpected needs.

**Evening review:** Which anchor was easiest to keep? Keep that one tomorrow.

---

## Day 3 — Make the next step smaller

**Today's focus:** Turn an overwhelming goal into an action you can finish.

**The larger goal:** __________________________________________

**Why it feels difficult:** ____________________________________

**A 10–20 minute next step:** _________________________________

**What I need ready before I start:** ___________________________

Set a realistic stopping point. Completing a small step is better than endlessly planning a large one.

**Evening review:** What did I finish? What is the next visible step?

---

## Day 4 — Protect your attention

**Today's focus:** Reduce one obstacle instead of trying to fix everything.

**One distraction or obstacle:** _______________________________

**A practical boundary I can try today:** _______________________

**A better use of that time:** _________________________________

Examples: put the phone out of reach for one short work session, close unnecessary tabs, prepare materials before beginning, or ask for a quiet period when possible.

**Evening review:** Did the boundary help? If not, choose a simpler one.

---

## Day 5 — Move a responsibility forward

**Today's focus:** Do one useful thing that has been waiting.

**The responsibility:** ________________________________________

**Why it matters:** ___________________________________________

**The first action:** __________________________________________

**When I will do it:** _________________________________________

If the task depends on another person, identify the part you can do yourself: gather information, draft a message, organize materials, or set a reminder.

**Evening review:** What moved forward, even a little?

## Day 6 — Practice gratitude through action

**Today's focus:** Notice blessings and respond with something beneficial.

Three things I appreciate:
1. _________________________________________________________
2. _________________________________________________________
3. _________________________________________________________

One person I can treat well, encourage, help, or thank: ______________________

One practical action that expresses gratitude: _____________________________

Gratitude does not require pretending that hardship is easy. It can coexist with asking for help and working to improve a difficult situation.

**Evening review:** What did I notice today that I might otherwise have overlooked?

## Closing reminder

A reset is not a magic fix, and this guide cannot promise a particular result. It is a way to pause, choose a manageable action, and begin again with greater intention. Keep the plan honest, practical, and kind.
